# UX Philosophy — Career Launch Holding Page

## Chosen Philosophy: The Announcement

**Organizing metaphor / mental model:**
A theater marquee between shows — the page is a single confident headline moment.

**How the PRD features map into this structure:**
- **Holding page:** the "returning for a new edition" headline is the star, given the
  visual weight of a poster (big type, brand color, generous space). "Explore LaunchPad"
  sits directly beneath as the one clear button; "See the 2025 impact" is a quiet text
  link below it; the data-deletion line is small print near the footer.
- **Stats access:** deliberately secondary — a quiet text link, not a feature of the
  layout. The stats pages themselves are unchanged.
- **Redirects:** every old sessions/booths link dumps everyone into this one moment.

**Trade-offs:**
- Good at: maximally on-message for "Stay tuned!"; preserves the primary/secondary CTA
  hierarchy (LaunchPad unambiguously primary); cleanest to render well in both the
  chrome-baseline and full-bleed variants.
- Sacrifices: utility density — visitors who came *for* something (stats, content) take
  one extra beat to find their path.

**Why the user chose this:**
Recommended by skill — user confirmed after initially wanting to render all three
philosophies for comparison. Chose to converge on The Announcement as the main
philosophy before Step 2; matches the client's copy energy and the chosen CTA hierarchy.

---

## Rejected Alternative 1: The Signpost

**Metaphor:** A wayfinding sign at a trailhead — the page's job is routing, not
announcing.
**Feature mapping summary:** Brief announcement line up top, then two visually distinct
"doors" (blue panel → LaunchPad, navy panel → 2025 stats) echoing the old site's split
Sessions/Booths hero; data-deletion line as small print.
**Trade-offs:** Routes every visitor type within a second and carries forward the 2025
site's visual DNA, but dilutes LaunchPad's primacy and reduces the announcement to
furniture.
**Why rejected:** Undermines the primary/secondary hierarchy the user and client chose;
announcement energy matters more here than routing density.

## Rejected Alternative 2: The Proof

**Metaphor:** A trophy case with a "back next season" sign — lead with what 2025
achieved, then the return.
**Feature mapping summary:** Two or three headline 2025 numbers as large stat teasers
linking into `/stats`, then the "returning" headline as payoff, LaunchPad CTA closing;
data-deletion line as small print.
**Trade-offs:** Strongest credibility play for sponsors, but requires sourcing and
maintaining real 2025 numbers on the page, reads retrospective rather than "stay
tuned," and inverts the announcement-first hierarchy of the client brief.
**Why rejected:** Content burden and retrospective tone conflict with the client's
forward-looking "Stay tuned!" brief.
